01 — The problems

What’s actually broken

Not a generic list. These are the specific complaints that come up in this trade, over and over.

  • High no-show rates, especially evenings and weekends
  • Clients price-shopping and waiting for a deal
  • Phones ringing constantly for availability
  • Online booking confusion between therapists
  • Schedule gaps that quietly destroy a week’s revenue
  • First-time client intake forms managed by hand
  • Feast-or-famine — overwhelming peaks, dead midweek

The pattern underneath

Almost all of it is the same three failures wearing different clothes: repetitive questions arriving through too many channels, appointments that leak, and follow-up that never happens because nobody has time.

Which is good news. If the problems are structural, so are the fixes — and structural problems can be built out of existence rather than worked around.

03 — Getting found

Local search and Google Maps

A booking system only helps once people find you. For a local business, that is mostly one asset — your Google Business Profile — and most of them are half-finished.

Why the listing decides it

When someone nearby searches for what you do, Google skips incomplete listings. No hours, no photos, no recent activity, no responses to reviews — and you are simply not in the shortlist, no matter how good the work is.

Getting that listing complete and keeping it active is the highest-return marketing work available to most local businesses, and it is not expensive.

The Eight-Step Fix

And for AI search

A growing share of “who should I use near me” questions are now answered by AI assistants rather than a map. Those answers are assembled from the same raw material: a complete listing, consistent business details everywhere they appear, real reviews, and clear pages that state plainly what you do and where you do it.

Everything we set up is built to be readable by both — the search engine and the model reading the search engine.

FAQ

Questions from this trade

How do massage clinics reduce no-shows?

Three things together: a cancellation policy applied at the moment of booking, reminders at roughly 24 hours and 2 hours out, and one-tap rescheduling. The last one matters most — most no-shows are people who couldn’t make it and found cancelling too awkward.

Can intake forms be collected before the appointment?

Yes — collected at booking and attached to the client record, so the first thing a new client does in your clinic is lie down rather than fill in a clipboard.

How do I fill midweek gaps in a massage schedule?

Existing clients first — automated rebooking prompts and a waitlist that auto-fills cancellations. Then new ones: a complete Google Business Profile, because a lot of midweek massage bookings are same-day searches from someone half a kilometre away.
